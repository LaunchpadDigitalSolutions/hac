CPR
Cardiopulmonary resuscitation
Call 999 now
If someone is unresponsive and not breathing normally

Around 30,000 people have a cardiac arrest outside hospital each year in the UK. Starting CPR immediately can more than double someone's chance of survival. You do not need to be trained to help — doing something is always better than doing nothing.

Adult CPR — step by step

1
Check for danger
Make sure it is safe to approach. Look for traffic, fire, electricity or anything that could harm you. You cannot help if you become a casualty.
2
Check for a response
Kneel beside them. Shake their shoulders gently and shout “Can you hear me?” If there is no response, they are unresponsive.
3
Open the airway
Place one hand on the forehead and two fingertips under the chin. Tilt the head back gently to open the airway.
4
Check breathing
Look, listen and feel for no more than 10 seconds. Occasional gasping is not normal breathing — treat it as cardiac arrest.
5
Call 999
Put your phone on speaker so your hands stay free. Ask someone to fetch a defibrillator if one is nearby. If you are alone, do not leave the casualty to find one.
6
30 chest compressions
Heel of one hand in the centre of the chest, other hand on top, fingers interlocked. Arms straight, shoulders over your hands. Push down 5–6cm and release fully each time.
7
2 rescue breaths
Only if you are trained and willing. Pinch the nose, seal your mouth over theirs, blow steadily for 1 second and watch the chest rise. If you are not trained, give compressions only.
8
Keep going 30:2
Continue until emergency help arrives, a defibrillator is ready to use, or the person starts breathing normally. Swap with someone else every 2 minutes if you can — it is tiring.

Hands-only CPR is fine If you are untrained, unwilling to give rescue breaths, or unsure — give continuous chest compressions and nothing else. It is far better than no CPR at all.

Using a defibrillator

A public access defibrillator (AED) will talk you through every step out loud. You cannot shock someone who does not need it — the machine decides.

1
Switch it on and follow the spoken instructions.
2
Expose the chest. Dry it if wet, and remove any glued-on patches.
3
Attach the pads as shown on the picture — one below the right collarbone, one on the left side below the armpit.
4
Stand clear while it analyses. Do not touch the casualty.
5
If it advises a shock, make sure nobody is touching them, then press the button. Resume compressions straight away.
Finding your nearest defibrillator The 999 call handler can tell you where the closest one is and give you the access code. Registered defibrillators across the UK are listed on The Circuit.

Children and babies

Children (1 year to puberty) ▼
  1. Give 5 initial rescue breaths before starting compressions
  2. Use one or two hands depending on the child's size
  3. Compress to about one third of the chest depth
  4. Continue at 30 compressions to 2 breaths
Babies (under 1 year) ▼
  1. Give 5 initial rescue breaths, sealing your mouth over the mouth and nose
  2. Use two fingertips in the centre of the chest
  3. Compress to about one third of the chest depth
  4. Continue at 30 compressions to 2 breaths

Common worries

What if I break a rib? ▼
Rib fractures do happen during effective CPR and they heal. A cardiac arrest without CPR is almost always fatal. Do not hold back — shallow compressions do not circulate blood.
Could I get in trouble for helping? ▼
Someone acting in good faith to help in an emergency is not held liable for the outcome, and no one in the UK has been successfully sued for performing CPR on a stranger.
What if they are actually breathing? ▼
If someone is unresponsive but breathing normally, do not start CPR. Put them in the recovery position on their side, keep the airway open and call 999.
